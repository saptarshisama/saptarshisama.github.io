Selling to Engineers (Without Lying)

August 5, 2025

Imagine you're debugging a system at 3 a.m., logs streaming across your screen, your code patch failing for the third time, and suddenly you see it. The one line, the off by one, the missing semicolon. You fix it, rerun, the system stabilizes. Relief floods in. The world makes sense again because the math checks out, the cause is linked to the effect, reality is preserved.

Or if you don't want computers, imagine standing in a workshop in 1905 with an engine in pieces on the floor. You reassemble the gears, tighten the bolts, pour the fuel, and when it sputters to life you know it isn't a trick. You earned it. The machine works because of the way atoms meet steel, not because someone told you a story.

That is how engineers think. They live in a world where truth is testable, where bullshit doesn't compile. So when you try to sell to them, the normal playbook of inflating the numbers, polishing the deck, or positioning the product fails. They can smell the lie instantly. They have been trained by reality itself, and reality doesn't negotiate.

The mistake most founders make is treating engineers like any other market. Engineers are allergic to hype. If you tell them your database is "10x faster," they will benchmark it themselves. If you say your tool "never fails," they will crash it within an hour. If you call your API "simple," they will know in 30 seconds whether it is or isn't. With engineers, words are weak currency. Proof is the only coin that spends.

So how do you sell to engineers? You don't sell, you show. You put the code on GitHub. You open your API docs. You let them try the demo without asking for their email. You let the product take the beating, because that is exactly what they are going to do anyway. And if your thing survives, they will respect it in a way no pitch ever could.

It is tempting to lie because lying feels like a shortcut. But with engineers, a shortcut is a dead end. The irony is that truth, shown clearly and plainly, is the fastest way through. If your product is good, it doesn't need hype. And if it is not, no amount of storytelling will save it. Honesty is not just a moral stance here, it is a competitive advantage.

The world already has too much noise. Engineers crave signal. Give them signal, raw and unembellished. Say what your product does, show how it works, let them verify. If it is real, they will use it and they will tell others. If it isn't, no pitch will help you anyway.

Selling to engineers without lying is not just possible, it is the only way that ever works. Because in the end, engineers don't buy the story. They buy the truth.
